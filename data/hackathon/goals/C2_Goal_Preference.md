## 4. Investment policy

**Return objective.** About 5% to 6% a year over inflation on investable assets over a full market cycle, to fund retirement at 60 and education.

**Liquidity.** $385,000 is ring-fenced for the rental down payment. After closing, keep at least 12 months of spending ($216,000) in cash or cashable GICs.

**Target allocation (financial assets, excluding real estate and partnership capital)**

| Asset class | Target | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash and short-term (outside the down-payment reserve) | 8% | 5% to 15% | HISA, cashable GICs |
| Canadian fixed income | 22% | 15% to 30% | Mostly in RRSPs to shelter interest income |
| Canadian equity | 20% | 15% to 30% | Eligible dividends in non-registered |
| U.S. equity | 30% | 20% to 35% | Mostly in RRSPs (no withholding tax on U.S. dividends) |
| International and emerging-market equity | 15% | 10% to 20% | |
| Alternatives | 5% | 0% to 10% | No direct real estate funds (already overweight) |

**Down-payment reserve.** The $385,000 stays in the non-registered account to keep the 90-day statement trail, moved into money-market holdings. High-cost-basis lots are sold first to limit capital gains.

**Permitted investments.** Broad ETFs and mutual funds, investment-grade bonds, GICs, individual large-cap stocks (with the limits below), donor-advised fund.
**Limits.** No single stock above 5% of financial assets. No leverage for investing. No new real estate beyond the planned second rental without a full review.
**Excluded.** Crypto, private placements sold to him through the law firm's clients (conflict of interest), tobacco.

**Rebalancing.** Review quarterly. Rebalance when any class is outside its range, using new cash first to avoid realizing gains.
